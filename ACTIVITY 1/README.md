# Activity 1: Presentation Design Principles

## Objective
Briefly describe the design principles applied in the presentation and explain how each principle improved clarity, engagement, and comprehension.

---

## Design Principles Applied

### Contrast
What it is: Contrast is the use of differences (color, size, weight, shape) to make elements stand out.
What I did: I used a high-contrast color palette for headings and important callouts (dark text on a light background), increased font weight for titles, and enlarged key figures.
How it improved the presentation: Contrast made the hierarchy of information clear at a glance. Viewers could immediately identify headings, important statistics, and next steps, which reduced cognitive load and improved retention.

### Repetition
What it is: Repetition is the consistent reuse of visual elements (colors, fonts, icons, spacing) to create cohesion.
What I did: I applied the same heading styles, bullet icon, and color accents throughout the slides and repeated a single layout pattern for content + image slides.
How it improved the presentation: Repetition created a consistent visual rhythm that helped the audience know what to expect. It strengthened branding and made navigation through the slides feel smooth and intentional.

### Alignment
What it is: Alignment is arranging elements so their edges or centers line up, creating an invisible grid that organizes content.
What I did: I aligned all text blocks and images to a common grid, used consistent left margins for paragraphs and bullets, and centered only elements that needed emphasis.
How it improved the presentation: Good alignment made slides look neat and professional, improved readability by creating clear reading paths, and made comparisons between elements simpler for the audience.

### Proximity
What it is: Proximity groups related items together and separates unrelated items to communicate relationships.
What I did: I grouped headings with their supporting bullets, placed captions next to images, and increased spacing between unrelated sections.
How it improved the presentation: Proximity made relationships between ideas obvious, reduced visual clutter, and helped viewers quickly scan and understand the structure of each slide.


---

## Reflection
Applying Contrast, Repetition, Alignment, and Proximity produced slides that were clearer, faster to read, and more visually appealing. The audience could focus on the main message without being distracted by inconsistent styling or clutter. For future presentations, I will prototype in a slide template (grid + defined styles) to maintain these principles from the start.

---

## Quick checklist for future slides
- Use a clear visual hierarchy for every slide (title, subheading, body).
- Apply the same heading styles and accent color across slides (repetition).
- Align text and visuals to a consistent grid (alignment).
- Group related items and separate unrelated ones using spacing (proximity).
- Ensure high contrast between text and background for readability.
